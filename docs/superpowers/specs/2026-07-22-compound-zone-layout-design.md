# Verb Endings Compound and Zone Layout Design

## Purpose

This document defines the spatial plan and production strategy for the first
complete *Verb Endings* experiment facility. It extends the arena and visual
design without changing the match loop or its core fiction.

The compound must work as both:

- three readable experiment zones used one at a time during normal rounds; and
- one connected, damaged survival space during the Collapse.

The first playable target is 8-12 concurrent players. Layout, replication
boundaries, spawn topology and scoring interfaces must support 24 concurrent
players without rebuilding the compound. Counts above 24 are outside the
initial product scope.

## Spatial Thesis

The facility is a faceted hemisphere organised around a central vertical core.
Its circular plan is divided into three wedge-shaped laboratories, each taking
slightly less than one third of the usable floor. Thick separator spines occupy
the remaining space and contain observation rooms, utilities, shutters and
concealed cross-zone routes.

An elevated annular Assignment Deck surrounds the Central Core. Subjects draft
verbs, form pairs and receive results on this ring before boarding Synced
Insertion Vectors. Ceiling rails carry each pair over the selected laboratory
and preserve player agency over the final landing point.

The compound uses circular geometry at the macro scale and a 2 m orthogonal
planning grid within each laboratory. The dome shell, deck ring and radial
junctions use repeatable 15- or 30-degree modules. Floors, doors, rails,
apparatus bases and dressing remain rectangular wherever possible. This keeps
the hemisphere legible without turning the environment into a collection of
bespoke curved assets.

## Compound Layers

### Assignment Deck

The Assignment Deck is the social and procedural hub. It contains verb
selection, pair drafting, results presentation, the researcher booth and
insertion-vector docks. Its elevated ring provides views into the three
laboratories and makes the facility's experimental purpose obvious.

Normal walking routes do not connect the deck to active experiment floors.
Insertion and extraction preserve match pace and keep the round reveal
meaningful. During the Collapse, the deck shutters and becomes inaccessible
rather than adding another combat tier.

### Central Core

The Central Core contains the Semantic Engine's visible infrastructure: plan
printers, power trunks, extraction machinery and the rotating insertion-rail
switch. It is sealed and orderly early in a match, then progressively exposed
through controlled state changes.

During the Collapse, authored breaches through the core become dangerous
shortcuts between laboratories. These routes are faster than the perimeter
network but expose players to severe machinery hazards.

### Laboratory Sectors

Each laboratory is one continuous open test floor. Apparatus islands and their
hazard envelopes create soft spatial regions without dividing the zone into
rooms or lanes. A player must be able to see the dominant apparatus during
insertion, land in several strategically distinct areas and traverse directly
between regions after landing.

Final dimensions are a greybox output, not an art assumption. The blockout must
test a provisional 48-56 m playable span per laboratory against movement speed,
camera framing, GROW clearance, knockback distances and 24-player density.

### Separator Spines

Three thick radial spines separate the laboratory sectors. They contain normal
experiment shutters, observation booths, service cavities and hidden
cross-zone passages. During early rounds these routes appear through windows,
vents, creature movement and inaccessible maintenance spaces. Later authored
damage may expose a pocket or briefly make one shortcut part of the active
round.

### Perimeter Maintenance Route

A concealed service route follows the inside edge of the dome behind the three
laboratories. It becomes the dependable long route between sectors during the
Collapse. The core shortcuts and perimeter route together ensure meaningful
navigation choices and prevent a single connector from becoming a choke point.

## Laboratory Mandates

The laboratories are not three visual skins over a generic arena. Each space
exists to measure a different claim about lexical power. Its apparatus,
circulation and damage history must express that mandate.

### Applied Causality Lab — Puzzle

This laboratory tests whether lexical traits can make physical systems produce
useful results.

Its dominant silhouette is an elevated result receiver fed by three incomplete
machine chains. Supporting apparatus islands initially cover:

- momentum and pendulum transfer;
- mass and counterweight control; and
- trajectory and gravity manipulation.

Floor markings, overhead cable runs and moving parts show cause, transfer and
result without relying on small labels. Landing near an easy first interaction,
a valuable downstream component or the flexible centre must all be viable.

Progressive damage crosses feeds, exposes improvised bypasses and turns precise
calibration equipment into emergency repair machinery. This is the first
vertical-slice laboratory because it exercises the widest reusable environment
vocabulary.

### Competitive Stress Lab — PVP

This laboratory measures dominance, displacement and resilience. It must read
as an instrumented test facility rather than an arena built for entertainment.

Its dominant silhouette is a reinforced impact crucible surrounded by
measurement pylons. Supporting apparatus islands initially cover:

- displacement and force plates;
- launch and knockback measurement;
- a resilience shelter; and
- destructible test barricades.

Low obstacles, apparatus and temporary fields reshape movement without forming
hard sub-rooms. Landing choices trade safety, elevation and proximity to
scoring apparatus. Damage reveals that failed equipment and subjects were moved
towards containment processing rather than treated as exceptional accidents.

### Adaptive Containment Lab — PVE

This laboratory tests survival against adaptive specimens, including previous
generations of Semantic Adaptation Units.

Its dominant silhouette is a central specimen-delivery column connected to
perimeter pens. Supporting apparatus islands initially cover:

- emergency shutter control;
- bait or attraction machinery;
- a defensive power node; and
- an observation habitat.

Enemies enter through pens, vents, wall cavities and service routes. They do
not appear through generic spawn effects. Landing choices trade defensibility,
escape access and early control of useful apparatus. Progressive breaches
reveal nesting failed subjects and make the programme's human cost physically
part of the level.

## Landing and Readability Contract

Every normal-round laboratory must satisfy the following rules:

1. The dominant experiment reads during insertion.
2. At least three landing strategies offer different advantages.
3. No valid landing creates an unrecoverable disadvantage or trap.
4. Players can cross the open test floor without passing through a single
   mandatory choke point.
5. Colour supports, but does not solely communicate, safe routes, apparatus
   state or experiment goals.
6. Puzzle-critical clutter stays at apparatus edges and leaves broad movement
   paths for up to 24 subjects.

## Round Progression

### Round 1 — Controlled

Only the active laboratory is live. Separator spines are sealed. Hidden routes
appear only as environmental evidence such as windows, vents and unreachable
service movement.

### Round 2 — Questionable

The first laboratory remains visible and damaged. A seeded inter-round event
may advance a named prop or reveal a small service pocket, but it does not open
free traversal between sectors.

### Round 3 — Containment Failure

The previous laboratories retain their authored consequences. One separator
shortcut may become usable as an advantage, hazard escape or creature breach
within the active round. This teaches that the sectors form one physical
compound before the finale depends on that knowledge.

### Inter-Round Event Scope

The first vertical slice ships with two seeded inter-round events across the
three-round match:

- one cosmetic or story event; and
- one event that advances a named prop or reveals a scoped service route.

Events are authoritative, discrete transitions selected from declared stage,
zone and prop prerequisites. The implementation must not introduce a general
cinematic system. Additional events are content expansion after the state and
replication mechanism is proven.

## Collapse Flow

The Collapse occurs when the Semantic Engine cannot produce a single first-
place result. Pair chains dissolve. Tied participants are launched individually
into fair, seeded insertion sockets distributed across all three damaged
laboratories.

### Phase 1 — Scatter

Participants receive a five-to-eight-second protected control window. Local
hazards wake while players orient themselves using knowledge acquired during
the normal rounds.

### Phase 2 — Connect

Separator shutters fail and the perimeter maintenance route opens. Every
occupied sector has at least two usable exits so the initial scatter cannot
create a spawn trap.

### Phase 3 — Hunt

Laboratory apparatus enters unsafe autonomous modes, previous subjects breach
containment and authored failures progressively remove safe space. The facility
contracts through comprehensible sector and apparatus failures rather than a
generic shrinking circle.

### Phase 4 — Core Failure

Dangerous shortcuts open through the Central Core. Sector failures proceed in
a seeded, authored order until one participant remains alive.

### Collapse Fairness Contract

The authoritative state ledger must guarantee:

- comparable initial hazard exposure across insertion sockets;
- no initial dead ends;
- at least one safe route between all surviving sectors;
- a dependable perimeter route before risky core shortcuts are required;
- clear geometric, lighting and audio telegraphs before lethal transitions;
  and
- deterministic resolution of simultaneous eliminations.

## Scene and State Boundaries

The compound should be one coherent topology from the player's perspective,
but its implementation remains modular:

- one compound root owns shared state and the Central Core;
- the Assignment Deck, three laboratories, separator spines and perimeter route
  are independently instanced scene units;
- each laboratory owns its apparatus, spawn sockets and zone-local presentation;
- gameplay collision remains in Godot scenes even when render meshes originate
  in Blender; and
- the server owns the run seed, state ledger and every navigation-affecting
  transition.

Internal culling or later streaming must not cause previously played sectors or
persistent damage to disappear when they should be visible.

## Modular Environment Kit

The first Blender-authored kit proves the structural language before hero
apparatus production. Its minimum set is:

- straight 2 m-grid floor panels;
- radial and annular floor segments;
- one repeatable faceted dome-shell bay;
- separator-spine shell and junction pieces;
- straight wall bay and corner or termination pieces;
- 4 m doorway and authored shutter;
- Assignment Deck rail and edge modules;
- support column and overhead truss;
- service vent or maintenance-panel module; and
- simple material-slot variants for clean, stressed and breached presentation.

All pieces use metre scale, applied transforms, predictable origins and stable
names. Structural pieces snap without mesh editing. Collision is authored or
wrapped in Godot rather than embedded throughout render meshes. Radial modules
are the smallest specialised subset; most production assets remain compatible
with the orthogonal kit.

## Production Plan

### 1. Scale Proof

Greybox the complete dome footprint, Assignment Deck ring, three sectors,
separator spines and both route networks with primitives. Validate basic
traversal, insertion sightlines, 8-12-player density and simulated 24-player
spacing. Lock provisional measurements only after this test.

### 2. Kit Proof

Configure Blender MCP and create the minimum structural kit against the approved
greybox dimensions. Export to GLB, import into Godot and verify scale, origins,
snapping, material slots, collision ownership and repeatability.

### 3. Applied Causality Vertical Slice

Replace one greybox sector with the kit and build proxy versions of the result
receiver and three apparatus islands. Preserve the landing and readability
contract while proving environment composition.

### 4. State Proof

Add controlled, questionable and containment-failure presentation to the slice.
Implement the two scoped inter-round events, one separator shortcut and a small
Collapse sequence. Prove authoritative state transitions before increasing
content volume.

### 5. Compound Expansion

Assemble Competitive Stress and Adaptive Containment from the proven kit. Add
only their specialised hero apparatus, containment pieces and necessary damage
states.

### 6. Full Collapse Proof

Connect every sector and validate fair scatter sockets, two-route connectivity,
hazard telegraphs and deterministic state order. Profile 24 networked actors
with representative hazards before visual polish or broader event authoring.

## Production Gates

Work advances only when the preceding proof answers its main risk:

- **Scale gate:** dimensions and sightlines work for the target population.
- **Kit gate:** pieces round-trip Blender to Godot and assemble without repair.
- **Slice gate:** one zone proves the art, apparatus and landing language.
- **State gate:** persistent damage and route transitions replicate correctly.
- **Collapse gate:** the connected compound remains fair, readable and within
  performance targets at 24 players.

## Out of Scope

The initial compound production does not include:

- sessions above 24 concurrent players;
- hard separation into parallel experiment cohorts;
- arbitrary structural destruction or runtime fracture simulation;
- unrestricted between-round exploration;
- a general cinematic or researcher-event framework;
- unique structural art for every sector and state combination;
- full art production before greybox scale approval; or
- hero-prop generation as the first Blender MCP task.
